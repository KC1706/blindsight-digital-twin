Robot cells are richly instrumented. Manual stations — where humans vary and the bottleneck usually hides — often have nothing but a checklist. MES/PLC dashboards are a rear-view mirror: they tell you the line stopped after it stopped.

9:14am: a torque tool drifts 4%. No alarm. Found at 2pm — hundreds of cars carry it.2
The idea

The line already knows. Read it differently.

  • Blocked / starved. A slow dark station starves everything downstream and blocks everything upstream — both visible at the instrumented neighbours. The boundary points straight at the bottleneck.
  • Travel-time tomography. Each vehicle's checkpoint-to-checkpoint time is one equation over the stations between. Thousands of vehicles → a system you can solve for every station's cycle time, sensor or not.

No retrofit. No new sensors. Just the line, finally telling you what it already knows.

Proof · real-time (new)

Live & recursive — it reacts, it doesn't wait for the shift

A Rao-Blackwellized particle filter fuses scans into a live per-segment posterior every tick, and snaps to a regime change — an operator swap, a tool drift — instead of crawling toward it.

Vehicle positions are pinned by scans, so the filter only tracks the thing that moves: each station's live rate.

Proof · mixed-model (new)

Different variants, different work — recovered with no sensor

Real lines are mixed-model: a sunroof on one trim adds work at one station. Blindsight recovers per-variant service times from travel-time tomography and localizes the extra operation to the exact dark station.

Honest limit: in a no-overtaking line only extra work is identifiable — a globally faster variant is pinned behind slower units. We say so, with bands.
